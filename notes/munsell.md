# Wada's colours in Munsell's system

Munsell's notation is how Japan's colour standards write a colour (JIS Z 8721), and PCCS is defined in its terms (see [pccs.md](pccs.md); PCCS was made by the Japan Color Research Institute, which grew out of the association Wada founded in 1927). Wada himself wrote colours in Ostwald's notation (see [ostwald.md](ostwald.md)), so nothing here is his: the notation is computed from our colour values, as a chip would be measured. The site's Colour systems › Munsell view shows it. [scripts/munsell.py](../scripts/munsell.py) computes it into `munsell.json`; the counts below are from the page.

## The system

- **Hue:** a circle of 10 families, R, YR, Y, GY, G, BG, B, PB, P and RP, each numbered 0 to 10, so 5R is the middle of red and 10R is 0YR. ASTM D1535 numbers the whole circle 0 to 100 (5R is 5, 5YR 15, ... 10RP 100).
- **Value:** lightness, 0 (ideal black) to 10 (ideal white), spaced to look even. The greys are N1 to N9.
- **Chroma:** distance from the grey of the same value, open-ended: as far as colours go.
- **A colour's notation:** hue, value/chroma, e.g. 5R 4/12; a grey is N and its value, e.g. N5.
- **The chips:** the 1943 renotation (Newhall, Nickerson and Judd) measured the system's colours under Illuminant C: 40 hue pages, 2.5 apart (2.5R, 5R, 7.5R, 10R, ...), values 1 to 9, chroma in steps of 2. Its 2,734 "real" colours are those a surface can reflect (within the MacAdam limits). Its Y is against magnesium oxide, the white standard of the time.

## Placing Wada's colours

- **Against the ideal white:** a chip's notation is measured against the perfect white, and Wada's colours are printed on paper, which reflects less. Our values (colors.json) are relative to the paper, as the CMYK was printed on it, so each colour's Y is multiplied by the paper's own: 0.8561, the media white point of the Japan Color 2011 Coated profile. Wada's white paper comes out N9.4.
- **Under Illuminant C:** each colour is adapted from D65 (CSS's white, our values') to C with Bradford's transform.
- **The conversion:** colour-science's (Centore's method, from the renotation data), with the value scale of ASTM D1535. Each colour's notation, converted back, is within 0.00008 of it in OKLab.
- **A colour's cell:** its nearest hue page, its value rounded (1 to 9), and its chroma rounded to the nearest even step, its ring. Under chroma 1 it is a grey: N and its value. The cell's code is what the view calls it, e.g. Munsell ≈ 5R 5/10; the exact notation is given too, e.g. 5.8R 5.4/10.2 for Eugenia Red - A.

## The system's own colours

- **Each empty cell** shows the renotation's colour for it, in our space: its Y scaled by 0.975 (magnesium oxide against the ideal white, as ASTM D1535 does), adapted from C to D65, and made relative to the paper. The greys N1 to N9 come from ASTM D1535's value scale.
- **Ranges,** as in the OKLCH view:
  - Screen: the chips Rec. 2020 holds, 2,097 of the 2,734;
  - Print: the chips the press prints (Japan Color 2011 Coated, at most 350% ink), 956;
  - all 9 greys in both.
- **A page's rings** at a value go out to its last chip in the range, or to one of Wada's colours, if that's further. A cell between the two has the renotation's colour, beyond the range, and says so: 2 cells in Screen, 33 in Print. Every cell has a chip in the renotation.
- **Their CMYK** is estimated through the print profile, as the OKLCH view's own colours are.

## The view

- **Flat:** the whole solid seen from above, every colour at once by hue and chroma, its value left out, as the OKLCH view's flat wheel. A page's rings go as far as at its widest value. Each of Wada's colours fills its page and ring (the greys their own grey's piece of the centre, which is shared among the system's greys, N9 to N1, lightest first clockwise from the top); an empty cell shows the chip of its hue and chroma at the page's widest value, or at the nearest value that has it: the most vivid colour of that chroma.
- **The disc:** one value of the solid, seen from above.
  - Angle is hue, the 40 pages anticlockwise as the hue numbers rise, as the OKLCH view's hues go: 5R near the right, 5Y at the top, 5PB at the bottom. The 10 families' 5s are named round it.
  - Distance from the centre is chroma, a ring per 2. The grey of the value is the centre, wider than a ring (0.12 of the radius, 1.9 rings) so that the first ring's cells, 9° each, aren't slivers.
  - The scale is Screen's: 14 rings (chroma 28) to the dashed rim, in both ranges, so that Print's smaller solid shows as smaller.
- **Cells:** as in the Ostwald view. Each of Wada's colours fills the cell of its code; the info panel gives the code (Munsell ≈ 5R 5/10) and the exact notation ("Measured"). Colours sharing a cell split it in hue order. An empty cell shows the system's colour, as the background draws it; hovering gives its code (Munsell = 10GY 5/12) and values, clicking opens its card. Picked in Create, it is saved as its OKLCH values and named by its code again when read back.
- **The control:** the greys N9 to N1 and the solid's outline beside them, at each value as wide as its widest page in the range, with the slice. Drag, click, scroll or use the arrow keys; Page Up and Down move 2 values, Home and End go to N9 and N1.
- **Switches.** The system (Ostwald, OKLCH, Munsell or PCCS, on the Colour systems page), Flat, 2D or 3D and Screen or Print, then the view's own, in every system's view in the same order and with the same icons. Space (real, or normalised), the background (the system's own colours, smooth, a grid, a grid with a dot in each cell, dots alone or none), its strength (full, or faded) and Wada's colours (on the grid, as dots or hidden), then the slice (2D) and the cut (3D), and last Reset. They're in a row above the disc, centred. Each view (Flat, 2D, the tone map, 3D) keeps its own background, strength, Wada's colours and space, and the page remembers them.
- **Changing system.** The switches' choices are shared by every system. Changing system keeps each choice where the new system has that switch and that choice. Where the system left had no such switch, or the new one hasn't that choice, it takes the switch's default. The defaults are 2D, Screen, the slice across the axis, the cut from the top, real space, a grid, full strength and Wada's colours on the grid, and they're what a first visit shows. So Print, then Ostwald (no range), then Munsell is Screen again, and PCCS's tone map, then any other system, is the slice across the axis. The level, the pages and the tone shown are each system's own. 3D's level is kept apart from 2D's and starts at the top, with the whole solid. The 3D camera carries over: the new system's solid is turned as far from its own starting side, and seen from the same height. A switch a system hasn't (Ostwald's space and range, the tone map's space and smooth background) is shown off, so that every switch is in the same place in every system, and the disc too (OKLCH's wheel is in a frame as wide as the other systems' discs). The switches' row is above the disc, where its size and the caption's length don't move it, and the slice keeps room for PCCS's third choice in every system, so the centred row stays put. A system not made yet is made when first chosen, the one shown staying until it's there. The choices made before the systems shared them carry over from the system last shown. Reset puts every switch back to its default, the peel too; the system, the levels, the pages and the camera stay.
- **Create's picker** works the same way, without 3D: the same switches in the same row above the disc, with Reset, drawn the same way, its choices shared by its systems with the same rule and defaults, and every switch in the same place in every system. Its choices are kept apart from the Colour systems page's. Picking from the grid and back keeps them. The choices it had before its systems shared them carry over from the system it showed last.
- **Pages:** the slice through the axis shows a hue's page on the right and the opposite one, 20 pages round, on the left (5BG and 5R first), the greys between, N9 at the top.
  - The cells are a ring across and 1.6 rings up per value (`TALL`). A ring and a value are about as far apart to the eye, as Munsell meant them, so square cells would be true to scale, but the solid then looks squat: the values are drawn 1.6 times as far apart, here and in 3D, so that its shape up the axis reads more easily.
  - Wada's colours sharing a cell split it side by side, out from the axis by chroma; greys sharing one, the lightest on the left.
  - The dial shows each page at its widest value, labelled with the 5s' codes (5R, 5YR, ...). Drag or click to the hue for the right, or scroll, or use the arrow keys (clockwise: right and down; Page Up and Down move 4 pages, to the next 5; Home goes to 5R). Buttons each side of the caption turn the pages.
- **3D:** the solid, drawn as the OKLCH view's. Its 40 pages are walls round a column of the greys, a cell a ring wide and a value (1.6 rings, as the pages) tall, cut at the value chosen, from the top or the bottom. Its background is the smooth walls (below), the smooth solid, a grid (the walls' edges as the cells' are, their cells filled and outlined), grid and dot, dots, or none, which leaves only Wada's balls. Each page has a button round it that hides its wall, or on the smooth solid opens its wedge. Hovering and clicking work as on the disc. The caption says what's shown, with the background, the peel, the wedges or pages hidden, Wada's colours and the space.
- **The smooth solid (3D).** The solid whole, its colour continuous, the field's colours (below) at every hue, value and chroma. It opens three ways at once. The cut takes off what's beyond the value chosen, and its face is that value in continuous colour, with a thin line round its rim. The peel, a vertical line on the control's outline, shows the shell where every colour is the same share of the way out from the greys to the edge, its outline dashed on the control. Drag it, or focus it and use the arrow keys, Home and End. A hue's button takes its page's sector out as a wedge, its faces the pages inside. Wada's balls in the peeled-away part or an open wedge stay, those inside are hidden by it, and those of the value cut sit on its face. Hovering gives the colour at that place ("Munsell, interpolated", with the nearest of the system's colours), clicking opens its card.
  - It's drawn on the GPU. Its edge (per degree of hue and 127 steps up) and colours (per 3° of hue, 63 steps up and 23 out to the edge) are sampled once into textures and interpolated between, so turning, cutting and peeling draw nothing again on the CPU. It turns at 60 frames a second, as the walls do, and takes about 0.3 to 0.4 s to make the first time, its textures kept for when it's made again.
- **The background.** Smooth is the continuous field (below). A grid fills each cell with the system's colour and draws its border as a thin grey line, which reads on light and dark cells alike. Grid and dot draws each cell as pale card with a dot of its colour, dots draw the dots alone, and none leaves Wada's colours with the range's outline. The pointer follows it, so on smooth it gives the colour exactly under it, on a grid or dots that cell's colour, and on none only Wada's colours. Faded draws it at a third of its strength, so that Wada's colours stand out.
- **Normalised space.** Every hue's edge goes out to the rim at every value, and each colour sits at its share of the way out to its own hue's edge. Across, a value is a full disc. Through the axis, every row is stretched to the same width, so each page is a rectangle. In 3D the solid is a cylinder. The range's outline is drawn in real space only. Wada's dots sit where they fall in the normalised space, or inside their cell on a grid.
- **Wada's colours.** On the grid, each fills its piece of its cell, split in hue order where several share it. As dots, each sits at its exact place, ringed white and dark so that it reads on any colour. In 3D they are balls, at their exact place, or in their cell's middle in the smooth solid when on the grid. Hidden, only the background shows, and with none too only the outline.
- **Smooth:** in Flat and 2D the solid is drawn as a continuous field instead of cells, its colours interpolated in OKLab between the system's own: out from the value's grey through the rings' middles, round between the pages' hues, and (pages) up between the values' middles. Where a page or value has fewer rings than the next, its outermost colour goes on, its slope eased to nothing at the last chip. Its edge goes round and up the same way, between the pages' edges in the range shown, at the cells' scale, its ends half a value beyond N9's and N1's middles. So its colours lie between the renotation's, and aren't its own.
  - Every step is a monotone cubic, round the pages as a loop and up the values held at the ends: the edge has no corners and the colours no creases, and neither overshoots its samples, so the edge never bulges beyond the system's colours. Out from the centre, a cubic that starts flat meets the rings' scale at ring 1's middle, so the centre has no point.
  - Each sample's slope is 4a²b² / ((a + b)(a² + b²)) from its two neighbouring differences a and b (none where they differ in sign), eased to nothing where both are below about a just noticeable difference. It keeps within Fritsch and Carlson's bounds, so there's no overshoot. Fritsch and Butland's harmonic mean, 2ab / (a + b), was used first, but its slope has a corner where a difference crosses nothing, and since the samples across (a page's colours between two values, say) change along the rings, that drew creases along the field, measured as slope jumps that didn't shrink with the step (0.023 OKLab per ring at a step of 0.01 and of 0.0025). With these slopes they shrink fourfold, as a smooth field's do (at most 0.001 at 0.0025).
  - Colours beyond what the screen shows (in Screen, on a narrower screen than Rec. 2020) have their chroma reduced at their own lightness and hue until they fit, as CSS maps colours, instead of each channel cut off. A line can still show where the field reaches the screen's limit, which no faithful mapping can avoid. In Print, or on a wider screen, it's smooth. The panel and the card give the colour's own values.
  - Wada's colours on the field are ringed white, then dark round that, with a shadow, so that they read as dots on light, dark and vivid colours alike, their fill their own colour.
  - Speed: the cubic's coefficients are worked out once per page, the field is painted at half a CSS pixel and clipped to its outline (so its edge stays crisp), each picture is kept (the last 32), and the other levels, or the pages either side, are painted ahead while the page is idle. A step between levels or pages takes about 4 to 7 ms once painted, and 20 to 45 ms the first time, against 120 to 250 ms before.
  - 2D and Flat: the field is painted on a canvas under the disc's svg, its outline over it. Hovering the field gives the colour under the pointer ("Munsell, interpolated", with the nearest of the system's colours), clicking opens its card.
  - 3D, the smooth walls. Each page is still a wall, with the gaps between them and the inside showing, but its edge goes smoothly up it (the pages' edge above, 512 steps up the wall), and its cells, each value's as many as in Cells and with the system's colours, are each the same share of the way out to that edge at every height, so that they follow it. The cut face, the page buttons, hovering and clicking work as with a grid.
- **Create:** Munsell is a source in the picker, flat or in 2D, as Ostwald is.

## How Wada's colours spread

- **Values 2 to 9:** none at value 1. Per value, 2 to 9: 7, 22, 29, 40, 18, 28, 17, 10.
- **Chroma:** up to 15.5 (8 rings).
- **Cells:** the 171 colours fill 159 cells; 11 are shared, by at most 3.
- **Greys** (under chroma 1), with their exact notation:
  - N9: White (N9.4), Pale King's Blue - B (5.9GY 9.1/0.7);
  - N7: Neutral Gray (3.4B 7.4/0.6), Mineral Gray (4.2BG 6.9/0.8), Pale Ecru-Drab (5.6YR 7.3/1.0);
  - N6: Warm Gray (4.5GY 6.5/0.6);
  - N3: Dull Purplish Black (3Y 2.6/0.6);
  - N2: Black (3.9Y 1.5/0.3).
- **Beyond the range:** one of Wada's colours is in a cell whose chip Rec. 2020 doesn't hold, and 25 in cells whose chip the press can't print. The colours themselves are within both; the chips, at the cell's rounded chroma, are further out.

## Limits

- **Our values are computed, not measured:** from the book's CMYK through the press's profile (see the README). The notation is as good as that.
- **The paper's white** is the profile's, not the book's paper, which isn't measured.
- **Illuminant C** is the renotation's: the adaptation from D65 is Bradford's, one of several, and changes the notation a little.
- **The renotation** is smoothed and partly extrapolated data, the standard for the notation, not a physical book of chips: real Munsell books hold fewer colours, and their chips vary.
